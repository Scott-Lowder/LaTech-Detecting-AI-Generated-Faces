# Fine-Tuning ResNet for Cross-Model Deepfake Generalization to Sora

## Abstract:
The rapid evolution of generative AI, epitomized by OpenAI’s Sora, poses a critical challenge to the reliability of
existing deepfake detection systems. This project addresses
the core research question: Can a deep learning detector,
pre-trained on older synthetic faces, effectively generalize
to and reliably detect the significantly more advanced faces
generated by the novel Sora model? We employed a twostage transfer learning methodology utilizing the ResNet architecture. Models were first pre-trained on the large Kaggle benchmark dataset (featuring StyleGAN faces). To isolate feature extraction on the face itself, a novel preprocessing pipeline was implemented using MediaPipe Face Mesh
to mask and remove all background variance. The resulting models were then fine-tuned on a small, manually curated dataset of high-fidelity Sora-generated faces, which
was heavily augmented to mitigate data scarcity. To assess generalization rigorously, we compared three ResNet
variants (ResNet-18, -34, -50) using 5-fold cross-validation
instead of fixed train-test splits, avoiding the unrealistically
high accuracies caused by memorization. Our final assessment revealed that while the deeper ResNet-50 architecture
exhibited the strongest performance with an average accuracy constrained to the high 80% range, larger models
were highly prone to overfitting. The results highlight that
the limited availability of novel data is the primary bottleneck, severely constraining the model’s ability to achieve
robust, real-world generalization against cutting-edge synthetic media.
